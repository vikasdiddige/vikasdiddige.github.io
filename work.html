Personal programming project · TU Bergakademie Freiberg · 2020

Isogeometric analysis: an end-to-end Python workflow

Developed a Python implementation of isogeometric analysis (IGA) for two-dimensional linear-elastic and coupled piezoelectric problems.

  • NURBS geometry: Created two-dimensional NURBS geometries directly from control points, knot vectors, weights, and basis functions.
  • Analysis setup: Implemented knot-vector and control-point connectivity, boundary conditions, mechanical loads, and coupled electrical degrees of freedom.
  • Solution procedure: Formulated and assembled the IGA stiffness matrix and force vector, including numerical integration and mappings between master, parametric, and physical spaces.
  • Python post-processing: Visualized deformed geometries and generated contour plots for displacement, reaction-force, and electrical-potential fields.
  • Verification: Compared mechanical and electromechanical results with Abaqus piezoelectric elements.

Master’s thesis · Porsche AG · 2021

Temperature-dependent visco-plastic model for crash simulation

Developed a temperature-dependent hypoelastic-plastic model with damage for SABIC polypropylene copolymer used in vehicle crash applications.

  • Constitutive modeling: Extended a TAPO-based framework with a modified hardening law, temperature-dependent Young’s modulus, and a Johnson–Cook thermal-softening formulation.
  • Temperature-dependent damage: Adapted the Johnson–Cook damage formulation using homologous temperature to capture temperature-sensitive damage and failure.
  • Calibration and validation: Calibrated and validated against tension, shear, compression, puncture, and three-point bending tests across three temperatures.
  • Component validation: Assessed predictive performance for a Taycan air-guide component.

Doctoral research · TU Bergakademie Freiberg · 2022–2026

Mixed finite-element modeling of hydrogen-promoted fracture

Developed a predictive phase-field chemo-mechanical framework for hydrogen-promoted fracture in metallic materials, with stress-assisted diffusion and hydrogen-dependent fracture resistance.

  • Mixed FE formulation: Formulated a mixed rate-type variational problem that treats hydrogen concentration (lattice occupancy) and chemical potential as dual fields alongside displacement and the fracture phase-field.
  • Chemical-potential field: Used chemical potential as the field governing hydrogen flux, enabling chemical-equilibrium boundary conditions at hydrogen-exposed surfaces without ad hoc concentration criteria.
  • Numerical advantage: Avoided the need for C1-continuous displacement elements and hydrostatic-stress extrapolation that arise in concentration-based diffusion formulations.
  • Modular implementation: Structured the formulation so that constitutive relations can be exchanged or adapted efficiently for different materials.
  • Computational efficiency: The rate-variational formulation yields a symmetric tangent matrix, allowing symmetric solvers to be employed for efficient numerical solution.
  • Implementation and verification: Implemented the model in an Abaqus UEL and verified it through benchmark examples, including hydrostatic-stress-dependent concentration evolution and sub-critical crack growth under sustained loading.
